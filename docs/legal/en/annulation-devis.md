# Cancelling or moving an appointment that has a quote

*Translation of the French version dated 12/11/2026 — the French version prevails. Official texts are quoted in French.*

*Last checked: 12/11/2026. Legal basis: Code civil (French Civil Code), articles 1113 to 1122 (offer and
acceptance), 1193 (modification of the contract), 1217, 1218 and 1224 to 1230 (non-performance, force majeure,
termination); Code de la consommation (French Consumer Code), L221-18 (customer's withdrawal). Texts word for word:
[textes/code-civil.md](../fr/textes/code-civil.md), [textes/code-consommation.md](../fr/textes/code-consommation.md).
Help sheet, not legal advice: in the event of a dispute, consult a legal professional.*

## Key points

A quote that has been **sent** is an **offer**: as long as the customer has not accepted it, there is no
contract. An **accepted** quote is a **contract**: it binds you, just as it binds the customer. What you can do
therefore depends on this difference.

| Status of the quote | What you can do | In Crénelio |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft** (never sent) | Delete it: it has no effect | « Supprimer » (*delete*) |
| **Sent**, no reply | **Cancel** it (withdrawal of the offer); before the end of its validity, at your own risk | « Annuler le devis » (*cancel the quote*); also offered when the appointment is cancelled or moved |
| **Expired** (validity passed without reply) | Nothing to do: the offer has lapsed | Status « Expiré » (*expired*), automatic |
| **Accepted** | Modify it or end it **only with the customer's agreement**, except in case of force majeure | Warning in the appointment window; new quote to be accepted |
| **Withdrawn** by the customer | Nothing: the contract is void, refund what was paid | Status « Rétracté » (*withdrawn*) |

## The words, and what they mean

Legal vocabulary distinguishes acts that everyday language all calls "cancelling":

- **Withdrawal of the offer** (C. civ. 1115 and 1116): you give up a sent quote before it is accepted.
  Crénelio displays it as « **Annulé** » (*cancelled*), the word used by quoting software and understood by the
  customer. Not to be confused with the **customer's** withdrawal (below).
- **Lapse** (*caducité*, C. civ. 1117): the offer falls of its own accord at the end of the validity period.
  This is the status « Expiré ».
- **Consumer's withdrawal** (C. consom. L221-18): the customer renounces the concluded contract, without giving
  reasons, within fourteen days. This is the status « Rétracté ».
- **Revocation** (C. civ. 1193): the contract is undone **by mutual agreement**.
- **Termination** (*résolution*, C. civ. 1224 to 1229): the contract ends because of a sufficiently serious
  non-performance (clause, notice or court decision), or automatically in case of permanent force majeure.
- **Delete**: only applies to a draft, which has no legal existence.
- **Cancelling an appointment**: simply organising your schedule; on its own, it does not affect the linked
  quote.

## Quote sent, not yet accepted

- You can cancel it. But an offer « ne peut être rétractée avant l'expiration du délai fixé par son auteur »
  (C. civ. 1116): before the end of the quote's validity, cancelling does prevent the contract from being
  formed, but **makes you liable** if the customer suffers a loss as a result (they had organised their day,
  turned down another professional…). Let them know and explain why.
- **If you do not cancel it**, the customer can still accept it, even if you have cancelled the appointment:
  the contract is concluded « dès que l'acceptation parvient à l'offrant » (C. civ. 1121). Their quote page then
  warns them that the job has been cancelled.
- **Appointment moved**: if the quote states a date of performance (« Le 04/12/2026 »), it no longer matches.
  Cancel the quote and send a new one with the new date. Stating a time frame (« sous 15 jours », *within 15
  days*) rather than a date avoids this situation.

In Crénelio: **« Annuler le devis »** button in the editor (optional reason, email to the customer), or the
**« Annuler aussi le devis »** (*also cancel the quote*) box in the appointment window when you cancel or move
it. The customer receives the « Devis annulé » (*quote cancelled*) email; their page states that the quote can
no longer be accepted and that no amount is due.

## Accepted quote

- « Les contrats ne peuvent être modifiés ou révoqués que du consentement mutuel des parties, ou pour les
  causes que la loi autorise » (C. civ. 1193). Changing the date, the price or the content requires the
  **customer's agreement**: agree the new date with them and, if the conditions change, have them accept a new
  quote.
- **Cancelling the job without their agreement** means not performing the contract: the customer may then
  demand performance, a price reduction, termination or damages (C. civ. 1217).
- **Force majeure** (C. civ. 1218): an event beyond your control, unforeseeable when the quote was made and
  whose effects cannot be avoided (accident, exceptional weather, closure of the site). If temporary, it
  suspends the contract; if permanent, it terminates it automatically. A mere increase in workload or a badly
  placed appointment is not force majeure.
- Crénelio **never alters an accepted quote on its own**: the appointment window reminds you of these rules
  when you cancel or move it.

## In the appointment window

When you cancel or move an appointment, the « Modifications à valider » (*changes to confirm*) summary shows:

- its **sent** quotes, with the « Annuler aussi le devis » box (unticked by default) and a reminder of articles
  1116 and 1121;
- its **accepted** quotes, with a reminder of the agreement required (1193) and of force majeure (1218).

The appointment's quotes are listed in its window, each one can be opened; « Nouveau devis » (*new quote*)
creates another one.
